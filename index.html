Research2022 – present

Reading neuroinflammation in situ, one cell at a time

In the De Jager Lab I design and lead multi-modal studies of T cell–driven neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease — imaging-based spatial transcriptomics (MERSCOPE, Xenium) read alongside single-nucleus RNA-seq and multiplexed immunofluorescence, across more than 150 postmortem human samples.

The work is end-to-end: cohort design and tissue through segmentation, scHPF factorization, cell-state annotation, vascular-compartment assignment, and ligand–receptor analysis of what microglia do inside a T cell niche. Every spatial signature is cross-validated against external reference cohorts — ROSMAP and SEA-AD — before it becomes a claim.

Before Columbia, seven years of in vivo neuroimmunology: conditional-knockout and amyloid mouse models, stereotaxic surgery, multi-route dosing, perfusion and CNS dissection, flow cytometry and qPCR — the bench half of the same question, asked in mice.
